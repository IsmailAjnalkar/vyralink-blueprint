Technical issue escalation

  1. Producer logs the issue from the card (opens an incident, O16).
  2. Producer tries the runbook fix: swap kit (O08), switch network line, restart encoder.
  3. Not fixed in 5 minutes: escalate to the on-call technician (phone and in-app).
  4. Not fixed in 15 minutes, or the LIVE is down: Location Manager decides to move booth, continue shorter, or end; brand is notified.
  5. Every step is timestamped in the incident.

Emergency stop · workflow

  1. Manager or producer selects Emergency stop request and a reason: safety, harassment, serious compliance breach, equipment hazard.
  2. Confirmation shows who is told: producer on the floor, seller (in-app alert), brand contact.
  3. The producer instructs the seller to end the LIVE in the TikTok app; Vyralink cannot end it remotely.
  4. Producer confirms the stop time; the session becomes “Ended early” and an incident is opened.
  5. Pay, fees and refunds then follow the contract terms for early ends.

Spec notes · O14MVP PHASE 2
Purpose. Watch every simultaneous broadcast at a location and act fast when something breaks.
Roles. Location Manager, Studio Producers (own sessions editable, others read-only).
Rules
  • Vyralink does not control TikTok streams. Starting, ending and in-stream actions happen in TikTok on the studio device; this screen tracks studio status and producer inputs.
  • Every number carries its source: Live (studio equipment), Delayed, Manual (producer entry, with time) or Unavailable. Never estimated.
  • Viewers, orders and GMV sync automatically only in Phase 2 and only where TikTok authorizes access; MVP is producer entry.
  • Cards sort by severity: interrupted, blocked, degraded, then normal.
  • Emergency stop is a logged instruction to the producer and seller, not a remote kill switch.
States and errors
  • Studio monitor offline → connection shows Unavailable with last update
  • No sessions → next session countdown
  • Producer hasn’t entered metrics for 30 min → reminder on card
